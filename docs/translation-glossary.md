# Translation glossary

The getting-started site is published in English, French, Spanish,
Portuguese and Swahili. English is the source. The other four were
written by machine and **have not yet been reviewed by a fluent speaker**;
each page says so until its language is marked reviewed.

This file is where a reviewer starts. It lists the terms the translations
use, so that one thing has one name, and says where each translation came
from:

- **EarthRanger**: EarthRanger's web app already shows this word in the
  language. A reader will see the same word there.
- **SMART 7.5.6**: SMART Desktop 7.5.6's own interface files have this
  text in the language. A reader whose SMART runs in that language sees it
  on screen.
- **not from a product**: neither product has it. The translation is the
  machine's choice and most needs a reviewer's eye.

To correct a page, and to mark a language reviewed, see "The
getting-started site" in [`web/DEPLOY.md`](../web/DEPLOY.md). If you change
a term here, change it in the pages too.

Portuguese follows the Brazilian usage of both products (*arquivo*,
*usuário*, *senha*).

## Doubts for a reviewer

A second machine read-through compared every page with its English page
and its corrections are in. It left these open, most of them in Swahili:

- **finding** (Swahili *tokeo*, *matokeo*): *matokeo* is also the everyday
  word for "results". The pages say *matokeo ya ukaguzi (findings)* at
  first mention. *Hoja*, or *jambo lililobainika*, may be clearer.
- **export and migration** (Swahili): SMART's word for Export is
  *Hamisha*, and the pages call migration *uhamishaji*. Where a page says
  to export again it adds *(export)*. Another word for migration may be
  better.
- **extract** (Swahili *toa data*): *kutoa* can also mean "remove". The
  pages add *(extract)*. *Dondoa* may be the usual software word.
- **subject** (all four): see note 4 above.
- **hold** (Swahili *kufuli*, French *réservation*, Spanish *reserva*):
  the time a running job keeps a load to itself.
- **run** (Swahili *utekelezaji*) and **load** (*uingizaji*, also used for
  "import"): understood, but not established terms.
- **ranger** (all four): *écogarde*, *guardaparque*, *guarda-parque*,
  *askari wa uhifadhi*. Usage differs by country.
- **Spanish** follows Latin American usage (*haga clic en*, *reportes*).
- **SMART's sign-in button**: see note 1 above.
- **The app's counts** (French, Spanish, Portuguese, Swahili): a few short
  parts such as "3 created" are shared by several nouns, so the past
  participle takes one gender (masculine) or, in Swahili, one noun class
  (*zi-*), and reads wrongly beside categories or choices (French,
  Spanish, Portuguese) or subjects and choices (Swahili).
- **deactivate** (Swahili *zima*, "switch off"): the app's word for
  making a choice or subject inactive (*si amilifu*).
- **configurable model** (Swahili *moduli inayosanidika*): not from a
  product.
- **hold** shown as a state beside a load's status: French *réservé*,
  Spanish *reservada*, Portuguese *com bloqueio*, Swahili *ina kufuli*.
  The Portuguese can be misread as "blocked".

## Findings that still show in English

A load's findings are stored in English and shown in the reader's language
where the app can tell which finding it is. These still show in English:

- a finding whose check could not run (code `check_failed`): its text is
  the check's name and a program error;
- a configured timezone that disagrees with the bundle's;
- every finding of a load made before release 0.13;
- a finding that a later release of `er-smart-sync` words differently or
  adds: it falls back to English until `web/findings.py` learns it;
- the detail lines and the copyable values of every finding. They are
  data: names, ids and permissions to type exactly.

Under each translated finding, "Original text (English)" opens the stored
wording, which also carries what the translation leaves out (a team's
members, how many patrols name it).
